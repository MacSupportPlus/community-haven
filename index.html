Built on ground with a history worth knowing

Established in 2017, The Community Haven sits on a 5-acre site at 815 North Hickory Street — once the St. Francis Catholic School for African Americans, later the Tennessee Correctional Workhouse, and later still the 28th Legislative District Community Development Corporation, which closed in 2012.

A legacy given a future

According to a 19th-century news account, Bushtown was the first Black incorporated township in the United States. Since 2015, The Community Haven has worked to give that legacy a future — through education support, workforce development, and affordable products and services for the neighborhood.

The gap we exist to close

Bushtown's poverty rate runs well above the national average — and that gap is the reason The Community Haven exists.

What we do in the neighborhood, every week

These are four of eleven programs. See all programs.

Community Patrol

Car, foot, and bike patrol focused on violence prevention, staffed by unarmed security personnel who also train community volunteers across the neighborhood.

Learn about the patrol

Feed & clothe the community

Canned goods and produce through our Food Bank partnership, plus a thrift store offering affordable, gently used clothes and household items.

See what's available

Youth development initiative

An eight-topic work-readiness series run over four weeks, delivered by facilitators twice a week in cohorts of ten — building real skills for teens headed into the workforce.

View the curriculum

Girls Academy & G.R.I.E.V.E.

A teen academy that advocates for girls affected by trauma, alongside G.R.I.E.V.E. — support and resources for families who've lost a loved one to homicide.
